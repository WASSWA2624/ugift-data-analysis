P46 Heading1 H I Executive summary
P47  Hgreen I The findings show a substantial stock of UgIFT assets supporting health care and secondary education, alongside equipment and buildings requiring attention. Facilities reported improvements in their services, but unfinished works, equipment out of use and weaknesses in asset identification limit the benefits. The report sets out the action needed to maintain the assets, complete outstanding works and resolve errors in the financial and custody records.
P48  Hgreen I A total of 229,024 asset records were identified. Schools account for 145,256 and health centres for 63,432. A further 16,663 relate to ministries and agencies, 602 to regional blood banks, 3,067 to hospitals under local governments and four to other local-government locations. The total includes items whose receipt, classification or programme assignment still requires confirmation. These cases must be resolved before the full population can be certified as physically verified UgIFT assets.
P49  Hgreen I Overall, 215,504 items (94.1%) are classified Functional and 13,520 (5.9%) Faulty, with the same numbers recorded as in use and out of use, respectively. Reasons for non-use include lack of power, unfinished buildings, missing parts and insufficient training. These require different responses. Usable tag details are unavailable for 178,695 items, making identification and custody checks a substantial task.
P50  Hgreen I Facilities reported improvements in services following UgIFT support. Of 284 health-centre respondents, 238 (83.8%) reported at least one benefit, including improved maternity services, access to care and use of equipment. Of 193 school respondents, 144 (74.6%) reported benefits such as better learning conditions and access to secondary education. These are the facilities’ accounts of changes in their services; the exercise did not measure how much of each change was caused by the programme.
P51  Hgreen I The programme list contains 629 distinct facility entries. Supporting returns account for 590, while 39 have discrepancies requiring reconciliation. The reviewed evidence identifies at least 70 facilities with incomplete works and 13 others awaiting handover or commissioning. Some facilities were operating while particular buildings or supporting structures remained unfinished. Completion, handover and readiness for use therefore need to be confirmed separately.

P53 Heading2 Hgreen I Priority actions
P54  Hgreen I MoFPED and the responsible institutions should first confirm when assets were placed in service. Of 201,066 full service dates, 3,345 precede programme approval on 27 June 2017. A further 27,958 entries contain partial dates or text, which cannot be accepted as exact service dates. The review also found 787 purchase dates later than the recorded service date. These findings overlap. Each affected date should be checked against receipt, acceptance or commissioning records, together with the asset’s ownership and programme assignment.
P55  Hgreen I Where a full service date cannot be supported, retain the reported period or status in the supporting records and leave the exact-date field blank until it is confirmed. A purchase date or the programme start date should not be used to fill the gap. Approve supported corrections before migration to IFMIS and recalculate depreciation and net book value where the corrected dates affect them.
P56  Hgreen I Available recorded costs total UGX 996.813 billion, with costs missing for 1,086 records. Net book value remains unresolved for 23,016 records. For the 180,730 recognized records with complete accounting figures, cost of UGX 760.987 billion less accumulated depreciation of UGX 165.185 billion gives a net book value of UGX 595.802 billion at 30 September 2026. Net book value is the recorded cost remaining after depreciation; it does not measure the asset’s market value or physical condition.
P57  Hgreen I Cost evidence also needs review. Costs for 166,593 items were estimated by comparison with similar assets. These account for 82.44% of available recorded cost. Of 298 construction-in-progress entries, 275 have zero cost and four have no cost recorded. The recorded amounts therefore cannot establish the full value of unfinished works. Obtain the supporting expenditure or valuation records, resolve missing amounts and approve changes before treating the balances as a verified programme valuation.
P58  Hgreen I Facility managers, local governments and the responsible ministries should give priority to essential equipment, reliable power and water, user training and outstanding buildings. Keep an action list naming the item or facility, the responsible officer and the agreed completion date. Assess faulty items for repair, replacement or disposal, and review the 22,161 fully depreciated records still marked in use. Reconcile delivery, transfer and custody records before concluding that an asset is missing. The recommendations and appendices provide the specific cases and follow-up required.

P62 Heading2 H I 1.2 Background to the verification
P63  Hred I The Uganda Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfers (UgIFT) Programme is a government programme implemented through a Programme-for-Results (PforR) launched and aligned with the broader IGFT Reform Programme (2015). UgIFT 1.0 began with US$200 million in FY2017/18, supporting Education and Health. This was later expanded through additional financing of US$300 million, including US$240 million in credit and US$60 million in grants, with US$50 million from the IDA Refugee Support Window, expanding beyond Education and Health to water and Environment and Agriculture (micro-scale irrigation) making the overall funding total of US$500 million. 
P64  Hred I The Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfer Reform Programme (IGFT RP) was revised in 2020 to align with the Third National Development Plan (NDP III 2020/21 - 2024/25), and to directly contribute to Four (04) Programmes i.e. Human Capital Development; Public Sector Transformation; Natural Resources, Environment, Climate change, Land and Water Management; and Development Plan Implementation. 
P65  Hred I Overall, the development objective of the UgIFT Programme was to: “Improve the adequacy, equity, and effectiveness of financing and the oversight, management, and delivery of Local Government services in Education, Health, Water and Environment, and Agriculture (micro-scale irrigation), including refugees and their host communities”. 
P66  Hred I Specifically, the Programme sought to: 
P67  Hred I Increase the adequacy and predictability of fiscal transfers to Local Governments for service delivery; 
P68  Hred I Improve the equity and transparency of allocation mechanisms through the introduction of formula-based transfers; 
P69  Hred I Strengthen performance incentives and accountability systems for Local Governments; and 
P70  H I Expand access to quality education and health infrastructure, particularly in under-served areas.
P71  H I The UgIFT Programme had four components aimed at strengthening the intergovernmental fiscal transfer system while supporting investments in service delivery infrastructure. These included;
P72  H I Component 1: Reform of the Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfer System
P73  H I This supported the design and implementation of a reformed fiscal transfer framework based on objective and transparent allocation criteria. This resulted into introduction of formula-based grants - Revised allocation formulas reflecting population, infrastructure gaps, and service delivery needs.
P74  H I Component 2: Education Infrastructure Development
P75  Hred I This component financed the construction and upgrading of education facilities, particularly government secondary schools in under-served sub-counties. This involved construction and equipping of new secondary schools - classrooms, laboratories, administration blocks, sanitation facilities, teachers’ houses, furniture and ICT equipment in the different local governments. 
P76  H I Component 3: Health Infrastructure Development
P77  H I This supported investments in health facilities to improve access to primary health care services at the local level. The major outputs under the component were construction and upgrading of health centers - Staff accommodation, sanitation facilities and provision of medical equipment.
P78  H I Component 4: Local Government Performance and Institutional Strengthening
P79  Hgreen,red I This aimed at Strengthening strengthening local government capacity in planning, budgeting, procurement, and infrastructure management. Under this component, Annual annual local government performance assessments have been undertaken across the 176 Local governments, assessments, capacity building and technical support strengthened monitoring and reporting systems across the Local local governments. 
P80  Hgreen,red I The Programme was implemented through a clearly defined results framework that connected actions and incentives across three levels of government to strengthen service delivery outcomes. The programme came to an end in on 31 December 2025 2025. and The had following accomplished areas of support describe the following outputs activities under the different components; current asset and facility findings are presented later in this report.

P150 Heading2 H I 2.8 Challenges and Limitations to the assignment
P151  Hred I Difficult terrain and poor physical accessibility of facilities: Physical accessibility presented a significant operational challenge especially for facilities located in areas characterised by difficult terrain and limited transport accessibility such as Bwesumbu HC III, Bwesumbu Seed School, Buhuhira and Kabingo in Kasese District. In addition, accessing facilities within the Island was difficult, time consuming and expensive. This led the teams to overshoot the anticipated time for data collection. 
P152  Hred I High transportation cost: Given the national wide coverage, the rising fuel prices led to high field costs. The field teams spent more money than was anticipated. The costs were extra ordinarily higher for island facilities like Kalangala, Buvuma, Mayuge among others. For instance, Kalangala had 3 facilities with long distance on water that required hiring a private boat since it was not a common route to the transporters and outside the ferry schedules. A similar situation was encountered in Buvuma and Namayingo districts. 
P153  Hred I Poor record keeping: In many LGs and facilities, there were no clear records about the assets delivered under UgIFT. The LGs and the facilities had incomplete or missing UgIFT asset registers which delayed data collection. Subsequently, the teams took longer in such facilities identifying the assets as most of these were not engraved with the UgIFT identifiers while others were not engraved at all. In addition, the field teams encountered situations where some Assets could not be physically accessed especially for schools since it was holiday time.

P504 Heading1 H I 5 Recommendations
P505  H I Recommendation. Facility managers and school governing bodies should keep the local and regional repair arrangements that already work and adopt a fixed routine: a condition check at least every school term in schools and every quarter in health centres, and a full count of UgIFT assets each year. Each facility should keep a condition book or fault list naming each item requiring action, the responsible person and the agreed completion date; the 36 facilities with no repair arrangement and the 80 with no functionality or breakdown record should set these up first.
P506  H I District health and education offices should review unresolved repairs each quarter and arrange technical assistance, and regional referral hospital workshops should include UgIFT equipment in their scheduled visits. Annual maintenance plans should separate minor repairs from major repairs such that the minor repairs be met from Primary Health Care funds or school grants, while major repairs can be handled through district or national funding.
P507  Hgreen,red I Recommendation. Facility managers, district health and education officers, engineers and technical teams should prioritise unsafe furniture, electrical concerns and failed equipment needed for essential services.
