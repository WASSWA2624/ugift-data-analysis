Facility managers, district health and education officers, engineers and technical teams should prioritise unsafe furniture, electrical concerns and failed equipment needed for essential services. Withdraw unsafe items, obtain technical assessments and verify safe operation after repair. The broken down and not functional assets should be assessed in order of their effect on care and teaching, beginning with clinical equipment and the school furniture that makes up most of the broken down assets; items beyond economic repair should receive a formal disposal decision. At national level, the Office of the Prime Minister should decide on repair or replacement of its three laptops recorded as faulty, and Uganda Blood Transfusion Services (UBTS) should repair or replace the six blood-pressure machines recorded as faulty at Arua Regional Blood Bank. Keep damaged assets separate from usable assets awaiting installation so that each receives the appropriate action.

500 Recommendation. LG Accounting Officers, engineers, health and education officers and contractors should coordinate outstanding buildings, utilities, furniture and equipment through site-specific completion plans for each of the 83 documented facilities with works or handover outstanding, comprising seventy with incomplete works and thirteen awaiting handover or commissioning. These are documented minimums. Installation should follow confirmation that rooms, power, water and security are ready. Stored assets described as new or in good condition should be issued to the rooms where they are needed, and each asset waiting for power or installation should have a named date for connection and testing. Protect services already operating during construction. There should be complete testing and signing off before handover or closing each action.

501 Recommendation. District health officers, facility managers, suppliers and regional technical teams should provide practical training for staff who use specialist equipment, starting with the Health Centres that linked idle equipment to a lack of skills, including oxygen equipment users, for example, at Bubago and Sop Sop and staff requiring orientation at Avogera. Handover should include a demonstration, basic maintenance guidance and a named route for technical support. Future supply contracts should make on-site demonstration to the staff who will use the equipment, a condition of acceptance and staffing reviews should take account of the equipment each health centre now holds.

502 Recommendation. Institution asset managers and LG finance, health and education officers should engrave all the verified assets with UgIFT markings and confirm their locations and custodians. Assets carrying another marking only should receive the programme identifier at their next check. Resolve the continuing allocation of equipment e.g at Todora, Paraa and Masafu through signed transfer or receipt documentation, bring assets held at district stores to their facilities once rooms are ready, and complete delivery of the items still with suppliers.

503 The Ministry of Works and Transport should retain the completed tracing record and keep the confirmed custodians and locations current. Losses should be reported and investigated under the public finance rules, and every facility should hold a list of the UgIFT assets it received, showing each item, its engraving and its custodian. Maintain site, ownership and handover records for buildings and land.
