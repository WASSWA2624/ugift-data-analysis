P44 Heading1  Executive summary
P45  NEW The findings show a substantial stock of UgIFT assets supporting health care and secondary education, alongside equipment and buildings requiring attention. Facilities reported improvements in their services, but unfinished works, equipment out of use and weaknesses in asset identification limit the benefits. The report sets out the action needed to maintain the assets, complete outstanding works and resolve errors in the financial and custody records.
P46  NEW A total of 229,024 asset records were identified. Schools account for 145,256 and Health Centres for 63,432. A further 16,663 relate to ministries and agencies, 602 to regional blood banks, 3,067 to hospitals under local governments and four to other local-government locations. The total includes items whose receipt, classification or programme assignment still requires confirmation. These cases must be resolved before the full population can be certified as physically verified UgIFT assets.
P47  NEW Overall, 215,504 items (94.1%) are classified Functional and 13,520 (5.9%) Faulty, with the same numbers recorded as in use and out of use, respectively. Reasons for non-use include lack of power, unfinished buildings, missing parts and insufficient training. These require different responses. Usable tag details are unavailable for 178,695 items, making identification and custody checks a substantial task.
P48  NEW Facilities reported improvements in services following UgIFT support. Of 284 health-centre respondents, 238 (83.8%) reported at least one benefit, including improved maternity services, access to care and use of equipment. Of 193 school respondents, 144 (74.6%) reported benefits such as better learning conditions and access to secondary education. These are the facilities’ accounts of changes in their services; the exercise did not measure how much of each change was caused by the programme.
P49  NEW The programme list contains 629 distinct facility entries. Supporting returns account for 590, while 39 have discrepancies requiring reconciliation. The reviewed evidence identifies at least 70 facilities with incomplete works and 13 others awaiting handover or commissioning. Some facilities were operating while particular buildings or supporting structures remained unfinished. Completion, handover and readiness for use therefore need to be confirmed separately.

P51 Heading2 NEW Priority actions
P52  NEW MoFPED and the responsible institutions should first confirm when assets were placed in service. Of 201,066 full service dates, 3,345 precede programme approval on 27 June 2017. A further 27,958 entries contain partial dates or text, which cannot be accepted as exact service dates. The review also found 787 purchase dates later than the recorded service date. These findings overlap. Each affected date should be checked against receipt, acceptance or commissioning records, together with the asset’s ownership and programme assignment.
P53  NEW Where a full service date cannot be supported, retain the reported period or status in the supporting records and leave the exact-date field blank until it is confirmed. A purchase date or the programme start date should not be used to fill the gap. Approve supported corrections before migration to IFMIS and recalculate depreciation and net book value where the corrected dates affect them.
P54  NEW Available recorded costs total UGX 996.813 billion, with costs missing for 1,086 records. Net book value remains unresolved for 23,016 records. For the 180,730 recognized records with complete accounting figures, cost of UGX 760.987 billion less accumulated depreciation of UGX 165.185 billion gives a net book value of UGX 595.802 billion at 30 September 2026. Net book value is the recorded cost remaining after depreciation; it does not measure the asset’s market value or physical condition.
P55  NEW Cost evidence also needs review. Costs for 166,593 items were estimated by comparison with similar assets. These account for 82.44% of available recorded cost. Of 298 construction-in-progress entries, 275 have zero cost and four have no cost recorded. The recorded amounts therefore cannot establish the full value of unfinished works. Obtain the supporting expenditure or valuation records, resolve missing amounts and approve changes before treating the balances as a verified programme valuation.
P56  NEW Facility managers, local governments and the responsible ministries should give priority to essential equipment, reliable power and water, user training and outstanding buildings. Keep an action list naming the item or facility, the responsible officer and the agreed completion date. Assess faulty items for repair, replacement or disposal, and review the 22,161 fully depreciated records still marked in use. Reconcile delivery, transfer and custody records before concluding that an asset is missing. The recommendations and appendices provide the specific cases and follow-up required.
P57 Heading1  1 Introduction and background
P58 Heading2  1.1 Introduction
P59   This report presents the findings of the UgIFT asset verification undertaken by the Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development as part of programme closure activities. It sets out the status of the assets provided by the programme to the targeted national institutions responsible for supporting the implementation of the programme, Blood Banks and local governments specifically targeting Seed Secondary Schools and health facilities upgraded from Health Centre II to Health Centre III. 
P60 Heading2  1.2 Background to the verification
P61   The Uganda Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfers (UgIFT) Programme is a government programme implemented through a Programme-for-Results (PforR) launched and aligned with the broader IGFT Reform Programme (2015). UgIFT 1.0 began with US$200 million in FY2017/18, supporting Education and Health. This was later expanded through additional financing of US$300 million, including US$240 million in credit and US$60 million in grants, with US$50 million from the IDA Refugee Support Window, expanding beyond Education and Health to water and Environment and Agriculture (micro-scale irrigation) making the overall funding total of US$500 million. 
P62   The Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfer Reform Programme (IGFT RP) was revised in 2020 to align with the Third National Development Plan (NDP III 2020/21 - 2024/25), and to directly contribute to Four (04) Programmes i.e. Human Capital Development; Public Sector Transformation; Natural Resources, Environment, Climate change, Land and Water Management; and Development Plan Implementation. 
P63   Overall, the development objective of the UgIFT Programme was to: “Improve the adequacy, equity, and effectiveness of financing and the oversight, management, and delivery of Local Government services in Education, Health, Water and Environment, and Agriculture (micro-scale irrigation), including refugees and their host communities”. 
P64   Specifically, the Programme sought to: 
P65   Increase the adequacy and predictability of fiscal transfers to Local Governments for service delivery; 
P66   Improve the equity and transparency of allocation mechanisms through the introduction of formula-based transfers; 
P67   Strengthen performance incentives and accountability systems for Local Governments; and 
P68   Expand access to quality education and health infrastructure, particularly in under-served areas.
P69   The UgIFT Programme had four components aimed at strengthening the intergovernmental fiscal transfer system while supporting investments in service delivery infrastructure. These included;
P70   Component 1: Reform of the Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfer System
P71   This supported the design and implementation of a reformed fiscal transfer framework based on objective and transparent allocation criteria. This resulted into introduction of formula-based grants - Revised allocation formulas reflecting population, infrastructure gaps, and service delivery needs.
P72   Component 2: Education Infrastructure Development
P73   This component financed the construction and upgrading of education facilities, particularly government secondary schools in under-served sub-counties. This involved construction and equipping of new secondary schools - classrooms, laboratories, administration blocks, sanitation facilities, teachers’ houses, furniture and ICT equipment in the different local governments. 
P74   Component 3: Health Infrastructure Development
P75   This supported investments in health facilities to improve access to primary health care services at the local level. The major outputs under the component were construction and upgrading of health centers - Staff accommodation, sanitation facilities and provision of medical equipment.
P76   Component 4: Local Government Performance and Institutional Strengthening
P77   This aimed at strengthening LG capacity in planning, budgeting, procurement, and infrastructure management. Under this component, annual Local Government Performance Assessments have been undertaken across the 176 LGs, capacity building and technical support strengthened monitoring and reporting systems. 
P78   The Programme was implemented through a clearly defined results framework that connected actions and incentives across three levels of government to strengthen service delivery outcomes. The programme came to an end on 31 December 2025. The following areas of support describe the following outputs under the different components; current asset and facility findings are presented later in this report.
P79 ListParagraph  Budget Support
P80   Education: 196 of 259 Seed Secondary Schools are complete of which 189 are operational. 
P81   Health: 354 of 373 health facilities, upgrades/new constructions (HC II to HC III) are complete.
P82   Water and Environment: 758 - piped water systems, 5,398- point water sources, 7,082 water supply systems rehabilitated, 325 designs completed, and 256 sanitation facilities in the targeted service units.
P83   Agriculture (Micro-scale Irrigation): Over 6,235 irrigation systems were installed (4,843 hectares under irrigation), 632 irrigation demonstration sites in 135 LGs set up.
P84   Refugee intervention: 51 primary schools and 35 health facilities in refugee hosting districts (RHD) were transitioned and integrated into the LG service.
P85   Blood Banks: Regional Blood Banks in Arua and Hoima were completed and commissioned while Soroti Regional Blood Bank was rehabilitated. 
P86   2.Grant management 
P87   Assets for institutional strengthening and retooling including ICT equipment and vehicles were provided. The Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development (MoFPED) through PFM Coordination Unit (RCU) manages the Government of Uganda counterpart funding of the programme to support the grant management component of the UgIFT programme. The grant management supports in financing of Central Government actions intended to achieve programme objectives and Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLIs). To this end, MoFPED through RCU supported MDAs to acquire different assets meant to support programme implementation. 
P88 Heading2  1.3 Justification
P89   The UgIFT programme came to an end on 31st December 2025. As part of the programme’s closure activities, MoFPED, through RCU, commissioned an end-of-programme asset verification exercise to take stock of the assets procured under the programme and to establish where those assets were, how they were being used as part of the processes to hand them over to government for sustainability purposes.

P114 Heading1  2 Approach and methodology
P115   The verification exercise was conducted using a participatory and consultative process and implemented in three interlinked phases: Preparatory and inception phase, Data collection phase and the report writing phase. 
P116 Heading2  2.1 Preparation
P117   After signing of the contract, the assignment commenced with an entry meeting on 28th May 2026. During this meeting, the Client specified target MDAs to be visited, the LG stakeholders to be reached as well as the documents to be reviewed. The Inception phase was completed and the inception report submitted on 18th June, 2026 which provided an agreed scope, approach and work plan for the exercise. 
P118 Heading2  2.2 Pilot Test
P119   The team also undertook a pilot test in Wakiso District Local Government, with verification carried out at Buloba Health Centre III and Sumbwe Seed Secondary School. During the pilot test, it was found out that, among others, there are cases of mismatches of facility names, that UgIFT assets can be integrated into the general LG asset register. This facilitated improving the design of the data collection process including; design of data collection tools, determining the timeline for data collection and estimated costs.
P120 Heading2  2.3 Data collection
P121   This exercise was undertaken in two phases first at National level and later at the Local government level. 
P122   National level: Physical visits to verify assets provided by the programme at the national-level was completed on the 19th of August 2026. This covered 15 MDAs including; Ministry of Works and Transport (MoWT); Ministry of Education and Sports (MoES); Ministry of Agriculture, Animal Industries and Fisheries (MAAIF); Ministry of Health (MoH); Office of the Prime Minister (OPM); Ministry of Water and Environment (MoWE); Ministry of Gender, Labour and Social Development (MoGLSD); Local Government Finance Commission; National Environment Management Authority (NEMA); Public Procurement and Disposal of Assets Authority (PPDA); Office of the Auditor General (OAG); Ministry of Public Service (MoPs); Ministry of Lands, Housing and Urban Development (MoLHUD); Ministry of Local Government (MoLG) and Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development (MoFPED). Additionally, three Blood Banks of Hoima, Arua and Soroti were covered.
P123   Local government level: The local government verification targeted UgIFT upgraded or constructed Health Centres and Seed Secondary Schools. The programme reconciliation list contains 629 distinct entries: 371 Health Centres and 258 schools under 160 recorded local government names. The process began with training of field teams prior to the actual field verification.
P124   Training of field teams: A total of 85 Research Assistants (RAs) and 7 Regional Supervisors were trained. The training took place from 21st to 23rd August 2026 while physical verification started on the 24th of August 2026. During the asset verification, the RAs visited Seed Secondary Schools, Health Centres and Blood Banks. 
P125   Physical verification: This exercise commenced on the 24th of August 2026 and was completed on 17th September 2026. The local government physical verification involved entry meetings with the LG leadership at the LG headquarters for authorization and review of reports relating to UgIFT assets, key informant interviews with the technical teams especially the Chief Administrative Officers, Finance Officers, District Health Officers and District Education Officers. Thereafter, the RAs proceeded to the facilities for physical verification of the assets.
P126   At local government level, the programme list had 629 facilities: 371 Health Centres and 258 Seed Secondary Schools. The list recorded 547 facilities (87.0%) as complete and 82 (13.0%) as ongoing. The ongoing works were more common among Seed Secondary Schools (63, or 24.4%) than among Health Centres (19, or 5.1%).
P127 Heading2  2.4 Quality assurance
P128   Regional supervisors checked daily field activity and reviewed the completed tools. The central technical team carried out spot checks and helped resolve operational questions. Teams compared institutional asset information with the items and explanations provided during visits, while supervisors referred matters requiring clarification to the team leader and field coordinator.
P129 Heading2  2.5 Asset categories, condition, use and identification
P130   Asset categories: Each entry in the Asset Register represents one recorded unit. The analysis covers buildings, ICT equipment, transport equipment, furniture and fittings, medical, laboratory and research equipment, other classified assets, items outside fixed-asset classification and unclassified entries. Assets are grouped by the institution or beneficiary to which they are attributed. 
P131   Condition and use: Condition and use were reported for each asset during the verification. A working asset is one recorded as functional and in use. An asset recorded as faulty was reported as not working and not in use. This included assets that were broken down or damaged. An asset that was new and in store was recorded as functional but not in use. Additionally, an asset that was held elsewhere, not yet delivered or awaiting power installation, completion of buildings or trained users, was also recorded as functional but not in use. 
P132   Identification: An asset is properly identified when carried a UgIFT programme marking. However, there were other assets that had no UgIFT marking but the officers on ground identified them as UgIFT assets.   
P133 Heading2  2.6 Financial analysis and Valuation
P134   The Government of Uganda Asset Accounting Policies and Guidelines 2023 was used to determine as the policy basis for determining recorded values, depreciation and net book values. Financial values are reported as at 30th September 2026. Straight-line depreciation was applied where the required information was available. 
P135 Heading2  2.7 Analysis and reporting
P136   Analysis and reporting was done through triangulation of the literature from different policy, Planning and Financial documents including: Government of Uganda Asset Accounting Policies and Guidelines 2023; asset registers; asset records at national and local government levels; physical inspections; discussions with facility managers; photographs and asset counts for each institution. 
P137 Heading2  2.8 Challenges and Limitations to the assignment
P138 ListParagraph  Difficult terrain and poor physical accessibility of facilities: Physical accessibility presented a significant operational challenge especially for facilities located in areas characterised by difficult terrain and limited transport accessibility such as Bwesumbu HC III, Bwesumbu Seed School, Buhuhira and Kabingo in Kasese District. In addition, accessing facilities within the Island was difficult, time consuming and expensive. This led the teams to overshoot the anticipated time for data collection. 
P139 ListParagraph  High transportation cost: Given the national wide coverage, the rising fuel prices led to high field costs. The field teams spent more money than was anticipated. The costs were extra ordinarily higher for island facilities like Kalangala, Buvuma, Mayuge among others. For instance, Kalangala had 3 facilities with long distance on water that required hiring a private boat since it was not a common route to the transporters and outside the ferry schedules. A similar situation was encountered in Buvuma and Namayingo districts. 
P140 ListParagraph  Poor record keeping: In many LGs and facilities, there were no clear records about the assets delivered under UgIFT. The LGs and the facilities had incomplete or missing UgIFT asset registers which delayed data collection. Subsequently, the teams took longer in such facilities identifying the assets as most of these were not engraved with the UgIFT identifiers while others were not engraved at all. In addition, the field teams encountered situations where some Assets could not be physically accessed especially for schools since it was holiday time.

P474 Heading1  4 Asset management 
P475 Heading2  4.1 Maintenance and repairs
P476   Maintenance keeps UgIFT assets working for the services they were bought to support. Most of the verified assets are furniture, clinical equipment and computers in daily use at Health Centres and seed schools, where wear and breakage are to be expected. Routine checks, a record of faults and a dependable route for repair decide whether a broken item returns to use quickly or stays out of service. During the verification, each facility was asked how it maintains, repairs, services or replaces UgIFT assets and whether it keeps a regular record of their functionality or breakdown.
P477   Repair arrangements. Of the 469 facilities that described their maintenance arrangements, 260 (55.4%) met repairs at facility level, from their own funds or with their own staff. Health Centres named Primary Health Care funds (94 of 278, 33.8%), while schools named the capitation or Universal Secondary Education grant, parents' contributions or the school budget (52 of 191, 27.2%). Busaale Health Centre III in Kayunga used Primary Health Care funds for repairs, and Kagumba Seed Secondary School in Kamuli had repaired broken furniture during the second term. A facility could describe more than one arrangement.
P478   Support from outside the facility was cited by 151  facilities (32.2%):  district offices, mainly the District Health Officer, 84  (17.9%);  regional referral hospital workshops or national teams 52 (11.1%); hired technicians and artisans 27 (5.8%); and contractors or suppliers under defects liability, guarantee or warranty 12 (2.6%). Major medical equipment repairs depended on this support. Todora Health Centre III in Nwoya worked through the District Health Officer and the Gulu Regional Referral Hospital technical team, Avogera Health Centre III in Buliisa received support from Hoima Regional Referral Hospital, and Bundimulangya Health Centre III in Bundibugyo obtained a team through the District Health Officer.
P479   In all, 97 facilities (20.7%), 59 of them schools, had not yet needed maintenance because the assets were new, not yet handed over or the buildings were still under construction, and 36 (7.7%) reported no repair arrangement, with broken items set aside or not repaired. Funds for maintenance and repair were reported as insufficient, particularly for major repairs, by 32 of the 505 facilities that described maintenance or challenges (6.3%). At Kyankaramata Health Centre III in Kyenjojo, Primary Health Care funds met minor repairs, but the cost of major repairs was a constraint.

P496 Heading1  5 Recommendations
P497   Recommendation. Facility managers and school governing bodies should keep the local and regional repair arrangements that already work and adopt a fixed routine: a condition check at least every school term in schools and every quarter in health centres, and a full count of UgIFT assets each year. Each facility should keep a condition book or fault list naming each item requiring action, the responsible person and the agreed completion date; the 36 facilities with no repair arrangement and the 80 with no functionality or breakdown record should set these up first.
P498   District health and education offices should review unresolved repairs each quarter and arrange technical assistance, and regional referral hospital workshops should include UgIFT equipment in their scheduled visits. Annual maintenance plans should separate minor repairs from major repairs such that the minor repairs be met from Primary Health Care funds or school grants, while major repairs can be handled through district or national funding.
P499   Recommendation. Facility managers, district health and education officers, engineers and technical teams should prioritise unsafe furniture, electrical concerns and failed equipment needed for essential services. Withdraw unsafe items, obtain technical assessments and verify safe operation after repair. The  broken  down  and  not functional assets should be assessed in order of their effect on care and teaching, beginning with clinical equipment and the school furniture that makes up most of the broken down assets;  items beyond economic repair should receive a formal disposal decision. At national level, the Office of the Prime Minister should decide on repair or replacement of its three damaged  laptops,  and Uganda Blood Transfusion Services (UBTS) should repair or replace  the six broken blood pressure machines at Arua Regional Blood Bank. Keep damaged assets separate from usable assets awaiting installation so that each receives the appropriate action.
P500   Recommendation. LG Accounting Officers, engineers, health and education officers and contractors should coordinate outstanding buildings, utilities, furniture and equipment through site-specific completion plans for each of the 115  facilities with works outstanding, starting  with the  22  of  them  that  were  not  yet  providing  services.  Installation should follow confirmation that rooms, power, water and security are ready. Stored assets described as new or in good condition should be issued to the rooms where they are needed, and each asset waiting for power or installation should have a named date for connection and testing. Protect services already operating during construction. There should be complete testing and signing off before handover or closing each action.
P501   Recommendation. District health officers, facility managers, suppliers and regional technical teams should provide practical training for staff who use specialist equipment, starting with the Health Centres that linked idle equipment to a lack of skills, including oxygen equipment users, for example, at Bubago and Sop Sop and staff requiring orientation at Avogera. Handover should include a demonstration, basic maintenance guidance and a named route for technical support. Future supply contracts should make on-site demonstration to the staff who will use the equipment,  a condition of acceptance  and staffing reviews should take account of the equipment each health centre now holds.
P502   Recommendation. Institution asset managers and LG finance, health and education officers should engrave all the verified assets with  UgIFT markings and confirm their  locations and custodians. Assets carrying another marking only should receive the programme identifier at their next check.  Resolve the continuing allocation of equipment  e.g  at Todora, Paraa and Masafu  through signed transfer or receipt documentation, bring assets held at district stores to their facilities once rooms are ready, and complete delivery of the items still with suppliers. 
P503   The Ministry of Works and Transport should retain the completed  tracing record  and keep the confirmed custodians and locations current. Losses  should be reported and investigated under  the  public finance rules,  and every facility should hold a list of the UgIFT assets it received, showing each item, its engraving  and its custodian. Maintain site, ownership and handover records for buildings and land.
P504   Recommendation. The Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development, with the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Education and Sports and the local governments concerned, should approve a corrected programme list after rectifying the inconsistencies. The corrected list should then guide decisions wherever planned locations differed from what teams found.
