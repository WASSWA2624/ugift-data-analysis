P45 [Heading 1] Executive summary
P46 [Normal] The findings show a substantial stock of UgIFT assets supporting health care and secondary education, alongside equipment and buildings requiring attention. Facilities reported improvements in their services, but unfinished works, equipment out of use and weaknesses in asset identification limit the benefits. The report sets out the action needed to maintain the assets, complete outstanding works and resolve errors in the financial and custody records.
P47 [Normal] A total of 229,024 asset records were identified. Schools account for 145,256 and health centres for 63,432. A further 16,663 relate to ministries and agencies, 602 to regional blood banks, 3,067 to hospitals under local governments and four to other local-government locations. The total includes items whose receipt, classification or programme assignment still requires confirmation. These cases must be resolved before the full population can be certified as physically verified UgIFT assets.
P48 [Normal] Overall, 215,504 items (94.1%) are classified Functional and 13,520 (5.9%) Faulty, with the same numbers recorded as in use and out of use, respectively. Reasons for non-use include lack of power, unfinished buildings, missing parts and insufficient training. These require different responses. Usable tag details are unavailable for 178,695 items, making identification and custody checks a substantial task.
P49 [Normal] Facilities reported improvements in services following UgIFT support. Of 284 health-centre respondents, 238 (83.8%) reported at least one benefit, including improved maternity services, access to care and use of equipment. Of 193 school respondents, 144 (74.6%) reported benefits such as better learning conditions and access to secondary education. These are the facilities’ accounts of changes in their services; the exercise did not measure how much of each change was caused by the programme.
P50 [Normal] The programme list contains 629 distinct facility entries. Supporting returns account for 590, while 39 have discrepancies requiring reconciliation. The reviewed evidence identifies at least 70 facilities with incomplete works and 13 others awaiting handover or commissioning. Some facilities were operating while particular buildings or supporting structures remained unfinished. Completion, handover and readiness for use therefore need to be confirmed separately.
P52 [Heading 2] Priority actions
P53 [Normal] MoFPED and the responsible institutions should first confirm when assets were placed in service. Of 201,066 full service dates, 3,345 precede programme approval on 27 June 2017. A further 27,958 entries contain partial dates or text, which cannot be accepted as exact service dates. The review also found 787 purchase dates later than the recorded service date. These findings overlap. Each affected date should be checked against receipt, acceptance or commissioning records, together with the asset’s ownership and programme assignment.
P54 [Normal] Where a full service date cannot be supported, retain the reported period or status in the supporting records and leave the exact-date field blank until it is confirmed. A purchase date or the programme start date should not be used to fill the gap. Approve supported corrections before migration to IFMIS and recalculate depreciation and net book value where the corrected dates affect them.
P55 [Normal] Available recorded costs total UGX 996.813 billion, with costs missing for 1,086 records. Net book value remains unresolved for 23,016 records. For the 180,730 recognized records with complete accounting figures, cost of UGX 760.987 billion less accumulated depreciation of UGX 165.185 billion gives a net book value of UGX 595.802 billion at 30 September 2026. Net book value is the recorded cost remaining after depreciation; it does not measure the asset’s market value or physical condition.
P56 [Normal] Cost evidence also needs review. Costs for 166,593 items were estimated by comparison with similar assets. These account for 82.44% of available recorded cost. Of 298 construction-in-progress entries, 275 have zero cost and four have no cost recorded. The recorded amounts therefore cannot establish the full value of unfinished works. Obtain the supporting expenditure or valuation records, resolve missing amounts and approve changes before treating the balances as a verified programme valuation.
P57 [Normal] Facility managers, local governments and the responsible ministries should give priority to essential equipment, reliable power and water, user training and outstanding buildings. Keep an action list naming the item or facility, the responsible officer and the agreed completion date. Assess faulty items for repair, replacement or disposal, and review the 22,161 fully depreciated records still marked in use. Reconcile delivery, transfer and custody records before concluding that an asset is missing. The recommendations and appendices provide the specific cases and follow-up required.
P58 [Heading 1] 1 Introduction and background
P59 [Heading 2] 1.1 Introduction
P60 [Normal] This report presents the findings of the UgIFT asset verification undertaken by the Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development as part of programme closure activities. It sets out the status of the assets provided by the programme to the targeted national institutions responsible for supporting the implementation of the programme, Blood Banks and local governments specifically targeting seed secondary schools and health facilities upgraded from Health Centre II to Health Centre III. 
P61 [Heading 2] 1.2 Background to the verification
P62 [Normal] The Uganda Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfers (UgIFT) Programme is a government programme implemented through a Programme-for-Results (PforR) launched and aligned with the broader IGFT Reform Programme (2015). UgIFT 1.0 began with US$200 million in FY2017/18, supporting Education and Health. This was later expanded through additional financing of US$300 million, including US$240 million in credit and US$60 million in grants, with US$50 million from the IDA Refugee Support Window, expanding beyond Education and Health to water and Environment and Agriculture (micro-scale irrigation) making the overall funding total of US$500 million. 
P63 [Normal] The Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfer Reform Programme (IGFT RP) was revised in 2020 to align with the Third National Development Plan (NDP III 2020/21 - 2024/25), and to directly contribute to Four (04) Programmes i.e. Human Capital Development; Public Sector Transformation; Natural Resources, Environment, Climate change, Land and Water Management; and Development Plan Implementation. 
P64 [Normal] Overall, the development objective of the UgIFT Programme was to: “Improve the adequacy, equity, and effectiveness of financing and the oversight, management, and delivery of Local Government services in Education, Health, Water and Environment, and Agriculture (micro-scale irrigation), including refugees and their host communities”. 
P65 [Normal] Specifically, the Programme sought to: 
P66 [Normal] Increase the adequacy and predictability of fiscal transfers to Local Governments for service delivery; 
P67 [Normal] Improve the equity and transparency of allocation mechanisms through the introduction of formula-based transfers; 
P68 [Normal] Strengthen performance incentives and accountability systems for Local Governments; and 
P69 [Normal] Expand access to quality education and health infrastructure, particularly in under-served areas.
P70 [Normal] The UgIFT Programme had four components aimed at strengthening the intergovernmental fiscal transfer system while supporting investments in service delivery infrastructure. These included;
P71 [Normal] Component 1: Reform of the Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfer System
P72 [Normal] This supported the design and implementation of a reformed fiscal transfer framework based on objective and transparent allocation criteria. This resulted into introduction of formula-based grants - Revised allocation formulas reflecting population, infrastructure gaps, and service delivery needs.
P73 [Normal] Component 2: Education Infrastructure Development
P74 [Normal] This component financed the construction and upgrading of education facilities, particularly government secondary schools in under-served sub-counties. This involved construction and equipping of new secondary schools - classrooms, laboratories, administration blocks, sanitation facilities, teachers’ houses, furniture and ICT equipment in the different local governments. 
P75 [Normal] Component 3: Health Infrastructure Development
P76 [Normal] This supported investments in health facilities to improve access to primary health care services at the local level. The major outputs under the component were construction and upgrading of health centers - Staff accommodation, sanitation facilities and provision of medical equipment.
P77 [Normal] Component 4: Local Government Performance and Institutional Strengthening
P78 [Normal] This aimed at Strengthening strengthening local government capacity in planning, budgeting, procurement, and infrastructure management. Under this component, Annual annual local government performance assessments have been undertaken across the 176 Local governments, assessments, capacity building and technical support strengthened monitoring and reporting systems across the Local local governments.

P148 [Heading 2] 2.8 Challenges and Limitations to the assignment
P149 [Normal] Difficult terrain and poor physical accessibility of facilities: Physical accessibility presented a significant operational challenge especially for facilities located in areas characterised by difficult terrain and limited transport accessibility such as Bwesumbu HC III, Bwesumbu Seed School, Buhuhira and Kabingo in Kasese District. In addition, accessing facilities within the Island was difficult, time consuming and expensive. This led the teams to overshoot the anticipated time for data collection. 
P150 [Normal] High transportation cost: Given the national wide coverage, the rising fuel prices led to high field costs. The field teams spent more money than was anticipated. The costs were extra ordinarily higher for island facilities like Kalangala, Buvuma, Mayuge among others. For instance, Kalangala had 3 facilities with long distance on water that required hiring a private boat since it was not a common route to the transporters and outside the ferry schedules. A similar situation was encountered in Buvuma and Namayingo districts. 
P151 [Normal] Poor record keeping: In many LGs and facilities, there were no clear records about the assets delivered under UgIFT. The LGs and the facilities had incomplete or missing UgIFT asset registers which delayed data collection. Subsequently, the teams took longer in such facilities identifying the assets as most of these were not engraved with the UgIFT identifiers while others were not engraved at all. In addition, the field teams encountered situations where some Assets could not be physically accessed especially for schools since it was holiday time.

P439 [Heading 1] 4 Asset management 
P440 [Heading 2] 4.1 Maintenance and repairs
P441 [Normal] Maintenance keeps UgIFT assets working for the services they were bought to support. Most of the verified assets are furniture, clinical equipment and computers in daily use at health centres and seed schools, where wear and breakage are to be expected. Routine checks, a record of faults and a dependable route for repair decide whether a broken item returns to use quickly or stays out of service. During the verification, each facility was asked how it maintains, repairs, services or replaces UgIFT assets and whether it keeps a regular record of their functionality or breakdown.
P442 [Normal] Repair arrangements. Of the 469 facilities that described their maintenance arrangements, 260 (55.4%) met repairs at facility level, from their own funds or with their own staff. Health centres named Primary Health Care funds (94 of 278, 33.8%), while schools named the capitation or Universal Secondary Education grant, parents' contributions or the school budget (52 of 191, 27.2%). Busaale Health Centre III in Kayunga used Primary Health Care funds for repairs, and Kagumba Seed Secondary School in Kamuli had repaired broken furniture during the second term. A facility could describe more than one arrangement.
P443 [Normal] Support from outside the facility was cited by 151 152 facilities (32.2%): (32.4%): district offices, mainly the District Health Officer, 84 85 (17.9%); (18.1%); regional referral hospital workshops or national teams 52 (11.1%); hired technicians and artisans 27 (5.8%); and contractors or suppliers under defects liability, guarantee or warranty 12 (2.6%). Major medical equipment repairs depended on this support. Todora Health Centre III in Nwoya worked through the District Health Officer and the Gulu Regional Referral Hospital technical team, Avogera Health Centre III in Buliisa received support from Hoima Regional Referral Hospital, and Bundimulangya Health Centre III in Bundibugyo obtained a team through the District Health Officer.
P444 [Normal] In all, 97 facilities (20.7%), 59 of them schools, had not yet needed maintenance because the assets were new, not yet handed over or the buildings were still under construction, and 36 (7.7%) reported no repair arrangement, with broken items set aside or not repaired. P461 [Heading 1] 5 Recommendations
P462 [Normal] Recommendation. Facility managers and school governing bodies should keep the local and regional repair arrangements that already work and adopt a fixed routine: a condition check at least every school term in schools and every quarter in health centres, and a full count of UgIFT assets each year. Each facility should keep a condition book or fault list naming each item requiring action, the responsible person and the agreed completion date; the 36 facilities with no repair arrangement and the 80 with no functionality or breakdown record should set these up first.
P463 [Normal] District health and education offices should review unresolved repairs each quarter and arrange technical assistance, and regional referral hospital workshops should include UgIFT equipment in their scheduled visits. Annual maintenance plans should separate minor repairs from major repairs such that the minor repairs be met from Primary Health Care funds or school grants, while major repairs can be handled through district or national funding.
P464 [Normal] Recommendation. Facility managers, district health and education officers, engineers and technical teams should prioritise unsafe furniture, electrical concerns and failed equipment needed for essential services. Withdraw unsafe items, obtain technical assessments and verify safe operation after repair. The Assets broken recorded down as and faulty or not functional assets should be assessed in order of their effect on care and teaching, beginning with clinical equipment and the school furniture that makes up most of the broken down assets; furniture; items beyond economic repair should receive a formal disposal decision. At national level, the Office of the Prime Minister should decide on repair or replacement of its three damaged laptops laptops, recorded as not functional, and Uganda Blood Transfusion Services (UBTS) should decide on repair or replace replacement of the six broken blood pressure machines recorded as not functional at Arua Regional Blood Bank. Keep damaged assets separate from usable assets awaiting installation so that each receives the appropriate action.
P465 [Normal] Recommendation. LG Accounting Officers, engineers, health and education officers and contractors should coordinate outstanding buildings, utilities, furniture and equipment through site-specific completion plans for each of the 115 83 documented facilities with works or handover outstanding, starting comprising 70 with the incomplete 22 works of and them 13 that awaiting were handover not or yet commissioning. providing These services. 83 cases are a documented minimum. Stored assets described as new or in good condition should be issued to the rooms where they are needed, and each asset waiting for power or installation should have a named date for connection and testing. Protect services already operating during construction. There should be complete testing and signing off before handover or closing each action.
P466 [Normal] Recommendation. District health officers, facility managers, suppliers and regional technical teams should provide practical training for staff who use specialist equipment, starting with the health centres that linked idle equipment to a lack of skills, including oxygen equipment users, for example, at Bubago and Sop Sop and staff requiring orientation at Avogera. Handover should include a demonstration, basic maintenance guidance and a named route for technical support. Future supply contracts should make on-site demonstration to the staff who will use the equipment, equipment a condition of acceptance acceptance, and staffing reviews should take account of the equipment each health centre now holds.

P485 [Heading 3] Rules for an approved register correction
P486 [Normal] Enter a full calendar date only when the evidence shows when the identified asset became available for use. Keep the source’s original wording and level of detail in the supporting record. A month, year, financial year or commissioning status must not be entered as though it were a full date.
P487 [Normal] Leave the date field blank in the corrected register where a full date cannot be supported. Record the reason, original value and source reference separately. Keep any work-in-progress or undelivered status visible even when the date is blank.
P488 [Normal] Where the source supports an early date, establish whether the asset predates UgIFT or has been assigned to the wrong beneficiary or programme. Do not simply move the year forward or remove the asset from the register. Record the approved decision about its ownership and programme assignment.
P489 [Normal] Before approving changes, compare purchase and service dates, check repeated entries and confirm which physical units they describe. A date may be applied to a whole batch only where the documents identify both the batch and the relevant event. Similar item names or dates at another facility are not sufficient evidence.
P490 [Normal] For each approved change, record the original and revised values, supporting evidence, decision, responsible officer and review date. Recalculate depreciation, net book value and any recoverable amount based on net book value. Retain independently supported recoverable amounts separately. Check the resulting balances and outstanding exceptions before migration to IFMIS.

TABLE 47
Term | Meaning in this report
Asset quantities | Each entry represents one recorded unit. Totals include capitalized, expensed and unresolved entries; receipt and ownership exceptions require confirmation.
Institution and location | Assets are grouped by the institution and beneficiary to which they are attributed. Transfers, substitutions and uncertain programme assignments require reconciliation.
Asset categories | The categories describe the existing accounting classification. Known errors require correction before the categories can be treated as confirmed physical inventories.
Recorded cost | The available cost in Uganda shillings. Some amounts are estimates. A missing amount remains unresolved and is distinguished from a recorded zero.
Service and purchase dates | The service date is when the asset became available for use. The purchase date is a separate event and does not establish the service date.
Useful life and salvage value | Useful life is the period used for depreciation. Salvage value is the amount expected to remain at the end of that period. Neither determines when an asset physically fails.
Depreciation | Accumulated depreciation is the cost allocated over the asset’s accounting life to date. Year-to-date depreciation covers 1 July to 30 September 2026. Pending calculations remain unavailable.
Net book value | Recorded cost less accumulated depreciation for assets recognized for accounting purposes. Comparisons use the same records with all required amounts available.
Recoverable amount | An independently supported recoverable amount must be distinguished from a value based on net book value. The latter does not constitute an independent impairment valuation.
Asset identification | Asset references, programme markings, serial numbers and vehicle registrations help establish identity. Shared identifiers and missing markings require physical and custody checks.
Condition and use | Functional and Faulty are the two reported condition classes. Reasons for non-use are examined separately to determine whether installation, repair, replacement or disposal is appropriate.
Complete accounting information | Assets classified CAPITALIZED or construction in progress, with cost, accumulated depreciation and net book value all available. These provide the basis for direct financial reconciliation.
