0044: p Executive summary
0045: p The findings show a substantial stock of UgIFT assets supporting health care and secondary education, alongside equipment and buildings requiring attention. Facilities reported improvements in their services, but unfinished works, equipment out of use and weaknesses in asset identification limit the benefits. The report sets out the action needed to maintain the assets, complete outstanding works and resolve errors in the financial and custody records.
0046: p A total of 229,024 asset records were identified. Schools account for 145,256 and Health Centres for 63,432. A further 16,663 relate to ministries and agencies, 602 to regional blood banks, 3,067 to hospitals under local governments and four to other local-government locations. The total includes items whose receipt, classification or programme assignment still requires confirmation. These cases must be resolved before the full population can be certified as physically verified UgIFT assets.
0047: p Overall, 215,504 items (94.1%) are classified Functional and 13,520 (5.9%) Faulty, with the same numbers recorded as in use and out of use, respectively. Reasons for non-use include lack of power, unfinished buildings, missing parts and insufficient training. These require different responses. Usable tag details are unavailable for 178,695 items, making identification and custody checks a substantial task.
0048: p Facilities reported improvements in services following UgIFT support. Of 284 health-centre respondents, 238 (83.8%) reported at least one benefit, including improved maternity services, access to care and use of equipment. Of 193 school respondents, 144 (74.6%) reported benefits such as better learning conditions and access to secondary education. These are the facilities’ accounts of changes in their services; the exercise did not measure how much of each change was caused by the programme.
0049: p The programme list contains 629 distinct facility entries. Supporting returns account for 590, while 39 have discrepancies requiring reconciliation. The reviewed evidence identifies at least 70 facilities with incomplete works and 13 others awaiting handover or commissioning. Some facilities were operating while particular buildings or supporting structures remained unfinished. Completion, handover and readiness for use therefore need to be confirmed separately.
0050: p 
0051: p Priority actions
0052: p MoFPED and the responsible institutions should first confirm when assets were placed in service. Of 201,066 full service dates, 3,345 precede programme approval on 27 June 2017. A further 27,958 entries contain partial dates or text, which cannot be accepted as exact service dates. The review also found 787 purchase dates later than the recorded service date. These findings overlap. Each affected date should be checked against receipt, acceptance or commissioning records, together with the asset’s ownership and programme assignment.
0053: p Where a full service date cannot be supported, retain the reported period or status in the supporting records and leave the exact-date field blank until it is confirmed. A purchase date or the programme start date should not be used to fill the gap. Approve supported corrections before migration to IFMIS and recalculate depreciation and net book value where the corrected dates affect them.
0054: p Available recorded costs total UGX 996.813 billion, with costs missing for 1,086 records. Net book value remains unresolved for 23,016 records. For the 180,730 recognized records with complete accounting figures, cost of UGX 760.987 billion less accumulated depreciation of UGX 165.185 billion gives a net book value of UGX 595.802 billion at 30 September 2026. Net book value is the recorded cost remaining after depreciation; it does not measure the asset’s market value or physical condition.
0055: p Cost evidence also needs review. Costs for 166,593 items were estimated by comparison with similar assets. These account for 82.44% of available recorded cost. Of 298 construction-in-progress entries, 275 have zero cost and four have no cost recorded. The recorded amounts therefore cannot establish the full value of unfinished works. Obtain the supporting expenditure or valuation records, resolve missing amounts and approve changes before treating the balances as a verified programme valuation.
0056: p Facility managers, local governments and the responsible ministries should give priority to essential equipment, reliable power and water, user training and outstanding buildings. Keep an action list naming the item or facility, the responsible officer and the agreed completion date. Assess faulty items for repair, replacement or disposal, and review the 22,161 fully depreciated records still marked in use. Reconcile delivery, transfer and custody records before concluding that an asset is missing. The recommendations and appendices provide the specific cases and follow-up required.
0057: p 1 Introduction and background
0058: p 1.1 Introduction
0059: p This report presents the findings of the UgIFT asset verification undertaken by the Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development as part of programme closure activities. It sets out the status of the assets provided by the programme to the targeted national institutions responsible for supporting the implementation of the programme, Blood Banks and local governments specifically targeting Seed Secondary Schools and health facilities upgraded from Health Centre II to Health Centre III.
0060: p 1.2 Background to the verification
0061: p The Uganda Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfers (UgIFT) Programme is a government programme implemented through a Programme-for-Results (PforR) launched and aligned with the broader IGFT Reform Programme (2015). UgIFT 1.0 began with US$200 million in FY2017/18, supporting Education and Health. This was later expanded through additional financing of US$300 million, including US$240 million in credit and US$60 million in grants, with US$50 million from the IDA Refugee Support Window, expanding beyond Education and Health to water and Environment and Agriculture (micro-scale irrigation) making the overall funding total of US$500 million.
0062: p The Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfer Reform Programme (IGFT RP) was revised in 2020 to align with the Third National Development Plan (NDP III 2020/21 - 2024/25), and to directly contribute to Four (04) Programmes i.e. Human Capital Development; Public Sector Transformation; Natural Resources, Environment, Climate change, Land and Water Management; and Development Plan Implementation.
0063: p Overall, the development objective of the UgIFT Programme was to: “Improve the adequacy, equity, and effectiveness of financing and the oversight, management, and delivery of Local Government services in Education, Health, Water and Environment, and Agriculture (micro-scale irrigation), including refugees and their host communities”.
0064: p Specifically, the Programme sought to:
0065: p Increase the adequacy and predictability of fiscal transfers to Local Governments for service delivery;
0066: p Improve the equity and transparency of allocation mechanisms through the introduction of formula-based transfers;
0067: p Strengthen performance incentives and accountability systems for Local Governments; and
0068: p Expand access to quality education and health infrastructure, particularly in under-served areas.
0069: p The UgIFT Programme had four components aimed at strengthening the intergovernmental fiscal transfer system while supporting investments in service delivery infrastructure. These included;
0070: p Component 1: Reform of the Intergovernmental Fiscal Transfer System
0071: p This supported the design and implementation of a reformed fiscal transfer framework based on objective and transparent allocation criteria. This resulted into introduction of formula-based grants - Revised allocation formulas reflecting population, infrastructure gaps, and service delivery needs.
0072: p Component 2: Education Infrastructure Development
0073: p This component financed the construction and upgrading of education facilities, particularly government secondary schools in under-served sub-counties. This involved construction and equipping of new secondary schools - classrooms, laboratories, administration blocks, sanitation facilities, teachers’ houses, furniture and ICT equipment in the different local governments.
0074: p Component 3: Health Infrastructure Development
0075: p This supported investments in health facilities to improve access to primary health care services at the local level. The major outputs under the component were construction and upgrading of health centers - Staff accommodation, sanitation facilities and provision of medical equipment.
0076: p Component 4: Local Government Performance and Institutional Strengthening
0077: p This aimed at strengthening LG capacity in planning, budgeting, procurement, and infrastructure management. Under this component, annual Local Government Performance Assessments have been undertaken across the 176 LGs, capacity building and technical support strengthened monitoring and reporting systems.
0078: p The Programme was implemented through a clearly defined results framework that connected actions and incentives across three levels of government to strengthen service delivery outcomes. The programme came to an end on 31 December 2025. The following areas of support describe the following outputs under the different components; current asset and facility findings are presented later in this report.
0079: p Budget Support
0080: p Education: 196 of 259 Seed Secondary Schools are complete of which 189 are operational. 
0081: p Health: 354 of 373 health facilities, upgrades/new constructions (HC II to HC III) are complete.
0082: p Water and Environment: 758 - piped water systems, 5,398- point water sources, 7,082 water supply systems rehabilitated, 325 designs completed, and 256 sanitation facilities in the targeted service units.
0083: p Agriculture (Micro-scale Irrigation): Over 6,235 irrigation systems were installed (4,843 hectares under irrigation), 632 irrigation demonstration sites in 135 LGs set up.
0084: p Refugee intervention: 51 primary schools and 35 health facilities in refugee hosting districts (RHD) were transitioned and integrated into the LG service.
0085: p Blood Banks: Regional Blood Banks in Arua and Hoima were completed and commissioned while Soroti Regional Blood Bank was rehabilitated. 
0086: p 2.Grant management
0087: p Assets for institutional strengthening and retooling including ICT equipment and vehicles were provided. The Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development (MoFPED) through PFM Coordination Unit (RCU) manages the Government of Uganda counterpart funding of the programme to support the grant management component of the UgIFT programme. The grant management supports in financing of Central Government actions intended to achieve programme objectives and Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLIs). To this end, MoFPED through RCU supported MDAs to acquire different assets meant to support programme implementation.
0088: p 1.3 Justification
0089: p The UgIFT programme came to an end on 31st December 2025. As part of the programme’s closure activities, MoFPED, through RCU, commissioned an end-of-programme asset verification exercise to take stock of the assets procured under the programme and to establish where those assets were, how they were being used as part of the processes to hand them over to government for sustainability purposes.
0090: p 1.4 Objectives of the assignment
0091: p The objective of the assignment was to undertake verification and recording of all assets acquired under the UgIFT Programme. Specifically, the assignment intended to;
0092: p Identify and verify the existence of the assets
0093: p Verify the assets condition and status: specifically, the condition on functionality, usage/performance and location among others
0094: p Assess financial management aspects: residual value of the investments/hardware/tools for purposes of financial reporting for project closure
0095: p List the assets that are obsolete, unserviceable and require disposal
0096: p Develop a computerized database of the investment/hardware/tools to facilitate recording of new deployed assets/investments.
0097: p Undertake PFM Assessment on Asset Management practices (including sustainability, Operations and Maintenance) of the UgIFT implementing LGs
0098: p 1.5 Scope of Work
0099: p The assignment covered institutions at national and local government level that directly implemented the UgIFT programme through coordination, support and implementation. At local government level, the study focused on upgraded health facilities and Seed Secondary Schools that received assets under the programme. Health Centers and other local-government assets are also reported separately.
0100: p Specifically, the assignment involved the following tasks;
0101: p Verification of the Asset’s Records of targeted ministries departments and agencies and local governments
0102: p Physical and Functional Verification of assets
0103: p Data Consolidation for generation of a single data base of assets provided by the program
0104: p Generation of an IFMIS ready Asset Register for Integration, and Migration into IFMIS
0105: p Review of the UgIFT Asset Management Practices
0106: p 1.6 Expected deliverables
0107: p Key expected outputs of the consultant include:
0108: p Report with the list and current status of assets acquired under the programmes;
0109: p An automated up-to-date assets data base of UgIFT Investments across MDALGs;
0110: p Report on the estimated value of assets under the programme as well as operations and maintenance arrangements;
0111: p IFMIS-Ready Asset Register and Data Migration Report
0112: p Report on functionality of assets relating to systems investments;
0113: p Report on Assessment of Asset Management Practices, Risks and recommendations. 
0114: p 2 Approach and methodology
0115: p The verification exercise was conducted using a participatory and consultative process and implemented in three interlinked phases: Preparatory and inception phase, Data collection phase and the report writing phase.
0116: p 2.1 Preparation
0117: p After signing of the contract, the assignment commenced with an entry meeting on 28th May 2026. During this meeting, the Client specified target MDAs to be visited, the LG stakeholders to be reached as well as the documents to be reviewed. The Inception phase was completed and the inception report submitted on 18th June, 2026 which provided an agreed scope, approach and work plan for the exercise.
0118: p 2.2 Pilot Test
0119: p The team also undertook a pilot test in Wakiso District Local Government, with verification carried out at Buloba Health Centre III and Sumbwe Seed Secondary School. During the pilot test, it was found out that, among others, there are cases of mismatches of facility names, that UgIFT assets can be integrated into the general LG asset register. This facilitated improving the design of the data collection process including; design of data collection tools, determining the timeline for data collection and estimated costs.
0120: p 2.3 Data collection
0121: p This exercise was undertaken in two phases first at National level and later at the Local government level.
0122: p National level: Physical visits to verify assets provided by the programme at the national-level was completed on the 19th of August 2026. This covered 15 MDAs including; Ministry of Works and Transport (MoWT); Ministry of Education and Sports (MoES); Ministry of Agriculture, Animal Industries and Fisheries (MAAIF); Ministry of Health (MoH); Office of the Prime Minister (OPM); Ministry of Water and Environment (MoWE); Ministry of Gender, Labour and Social Development (MoGLSD); Local Government Finance Commission; National Environment Management Authority (NEMA); Public Procurement and Disposal of Assets Authority (PPDA); Office of the Auditor General (OAG); Ministry of Public Service (MoPs); Ministry of Lands, Housing and Urban Development (MoLHUD); Ministry of Local Government (MoLG) and Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development (MoFPED). Additionally, three Blood Banks of Hoima, Arua and Soroti were covered.
0123: p Local government level: The local government verification targeted UgIFT upgraded or constructed Health Centres and Seed Secondary Schools. The programme reconciliation list contains 629 distinct entries: 371 Health Centres and 258 schools under 160 recorded local government names. The process began with training of field teams prior to the actual field verification.
0124: p Training of field teams: A total of 85 Research Assistants (RAs) and 7 Regional Supervisors were trained. The training took place from 21st to 23rd August 2026 while physical verification started on the 24th of August 2026. During the asset verification, the RAs visited Seed Secondary Schools, Health Centres and Blood Banks.
0125: p Physical verification: This exercise commenced on the 24th of August 2026 and was completed on 17th September 2026. The local government physical verification involved entry meetings with the LG leadership at the LG headquarters for authorization and review of reports relating to UgIFT assets, key informant interviews with the technical teams especially the Chief Administrative Officers, Finance Officers, District Health Officers and District Education Officers. Thereafter, the RAs proceeded to the facilities for physical verification of the assets.
0126: p At local government level, the programme list had 629 facilities: 371 Health Centres and 258 Seed Secondary Schools. The list recorded 547 facilities (87.0%) as complete and 82 (13.0%) as ongoing. The ongoing works were more common among Seed Secondary Schools (63, or 24.4%) than among Health Centres (19, or 5.1%). 
0127: p 2.4 Quality assurance
0128: p Regional supervisors checked daily field activity and reviewed the completed tools. The central technical team carried out spot checks and helped resolve operational questions. Teams compared institutional asset information with the items and explanations provided during visits, while supervisors referred matters requiring clarification to the team leader and field coordinator.
0129: p 2.5 Asset categories, condition, use and identification
0130: p Asset categories: Each entry in the Asset Register represents one recorded unit. The analysis covers buildings, ICT equipment, transport equipment, furniture and fittings, medical, laboratory and research equipment, other classified assets, items outside fixed-asset classification and unclassified entries. Assets are grouped by the institution or beneficiary to which they are attributed. 
0131: p Condition and use: Condition and use were reported for each asset during the verification. A working asset is one recorded as functional and in use. An asset recorded as faulty was reported as not working and not in use. This included assets that were broken down or damaged. An asset that was new and in store was recorded as functional but not in use. Additionally, an asset that was held elsewhere, not yet delivered or awaiting power installation, completion of buildings or trained users, was also recorded as functional but not in use.
0132: p Identification: An asset is properly identified when carried a UgIFT programme marking. However, there were other assets that had no UgIFT marking but the officers on ground identified them as UgIFT assets.   
0133: p 2.6 Financial analysis and Valuation
0134: p The Government of Uganda Asset Accounting Policies and Guidelines 2023 was used to determine as the policy basis for determining recorded values, depreciation and net book values. Financial values are reported as at 30th September 2026. Straight-line depreciation was applied where the required information was available. 
0135: p 2.7 Analysis and reporting
0136: p Analysis and reporting was done through triangulation of the literature from different policy, Planning and Financial documents including: Government of Uganda Asset Accounting Policies and Guidelines 2023; asset registers; asset records at national and local government levels; physical inspections; discussions with facility managers; photographs and asset counts for each institution.
0137: p 2.8 Challenges and Limitations to the assignment
0138: p Difficult terrain and poor physical accessibility of facilities: Physical accessibility presented a significant operational challenge especially for facilities located in areas characterised by difficult terrain and limited transport accessibility such as Bwesumbu HC III, Bwesumbu Seed School, Buhuhira and Kabingo in Kasese District. In addition, accessing facilities within the Island was difficult, time consuming and expensive. This led the teams to overshoot the anticipated time for data collection.
0139: p High transportation cost: Given the national wide coverage, the rising fuel prices led to high field costs. The field teams spent more money than was anticipated. The costs were extra ordinarily higher for island facilities like Kalangala, Buvuma, Mayuge among others. For instance, Kalangala had 3 facilities with long distance on water that required hiring a private boat since it was not a common route to the transporters and outside the ferry schedules. A similar situation was encountered in Buvuma and Namayingo districts.
0140: p Poor record keeping: In many LGs and facilities, there were no clear records about the assets delivered under UgIFT. The LGs and the facilities had incomplete or missing UgIFT asset registers which delayed data collection. Subsequently, the teams took longer in such facilities identifying the assets as most of these were not engraved with the UgIFT identifiers while others were not engraved at all. In addition, the field teams encountered situations where some Assets could not be physically accessed especially for schools since it was holiday time.

0474: p 4 Asset management
0475: p 4.1 Maintenance and repairs
0476: p Maintenance keeps UgIFT assets working for the services they were bought to support. Most of the verified assets are furniture, clinical equipment and computers in daily use at Health Centres and seed schools, where wear and breakage are to be expected. Routine checks, a record of faults and a dependable route for repair decide whether a broken item returns to use quickly or stays out of service. During the verification, each facility was asked how it maintains, repairs, services or replaces UgIFT assets and whether it keeps a regular record of their functionality or breakdown.
0477: p Repair arrangements. Of the 469 facilities that described their maintenance arrangements, 260 (55.4%) met repairs at facility level, from their own funds or with their own staff. Health Centres named Primary Health Care funds (94 of 278, 33.8%), while schools named the capitation or Universal Secondary Education grant, parents' contributions or the school budget (52 of 191, 27.2%). Busaale Health Centre III in Kayunga used Primary Health Care funds for repairs, and Kagumba Seed Secondary School in Kamuli had repaired broken furniture during the second term. A facility could describe more than one arrangement.
0478: p Support from outside the facility was cited by  152 facilities  (32.4%): district offices, mainly the District Health Officer,  85  (18.1%); regional referral hospital workshops or national teams 52 (11.1%); hired technicians and artisans 27 (5.8%); and contractors or suppliers under defects liability, guarantee or warranty 12 (2.6%). Major medical equipment repairs depended on this support. Todora Health Centre III in Nwoya worked through the District Health Officer and the Gulu Regional Referral Hospital technical team, Avogera Health Centre III in Buliisa received support from Hoima Regional Referral Hospital, and Bundimulangya Health Centre III in Bundibugyo obtained a team through the District Health Officer.
0479: p In all, 97 facilities (20.7%), 59 of them schools, had not yet needed maintenance because the assets were new, not yet handed over or the buildings were still under construction, and 36 (7.7%) reported no repair arrangement, with broken items set aside or not repaired. Funds for maintenance and repair were reported as insufficient, particularly for major repairs, by 32 of the 505 facilities that described maintenance or challenges (6.3%). At Kyankaramata Health Centre III in Kyenjojo, Primary Health Care funds met minor repairs, but the cost of major repairs was a constraint.

0496: p 5 Recommendations
0497: p Recommendation. Facility managers and school governing bodies should keep the local and regional repair arrangements that already work and adopt a fixed routine: a condition check at least every school term in schools and every quarter in health centres, and a full count of UgIFT assets each year. Each facility should keep a condition book or fault list naming each item requiring action, the responsible person and the agreed completion date; the 36 facilities with no repair arrangement and the 80 with no functionality or breakdown record should set these up first.
0498: p District health and education offices should review unresolved repairs each quarter and arrange technical assistance, and regional referral hospital workshops should include UgIFT equipment in their scheduled visits. Annual maintenance plans should separate minor repairs from major repairs such that the minor repairs be met from Primary Health Care funds or school grants, while major repairs can be handled through district or national funding.
0499: p Recommendation. Facility managers, district health and education officers, engineers and technical teams should prioritise unsafe furniture, electrical concerns and failed equipment needed for essential services. Withdraw unsafe items, obtain technical assessments and verify safe operation after repair.  Assets  recorded  as  faulty or not functional should be assessed in order of their effect on care and teaching, beginning with clinical equipment and school  furniture; items beyond economic repair should receive a formal disposal decision. At national level, the Office of the Prime Minister should decide on repair or replacement of its three  laptops  recorded as not functional, and Uganda Blood Transfusion Services (UBTS) should decide on repair or  replacement of the six blood pressure machines recorded as not functional at Arua Regional Blood Bank. Keep damaged assets separate from usable assets awaiting installation so that each receives the appropriate action.
0500: p Recommendation. LG Accounting Officers, engineers, health and education officers and contractors should coordinate outstanding buildings, utilities, furniture and equipment through site-specific completion plans for each of the  83 documented facilities with works or handover outstanding,  comprising 70 with  incomplete  works  and  13  awaiting  handover  or  commissioning.  These  83 cases are a documented minimum. Installation should follow confirmation that rooms, power, water and security are ready. Stored assets described as new or in good condition should be issued to the rooms where they are needed, and each asset waiting for power or installation should have a named date for connection and testing. Protect services already operating during construction. There should be complete testing and signing off before handover or closing each action.
0501: p Recommendation. District health officers, facility managers, suppliers and regional technical teams should provide practical training for staff who use specialist equipment, starting with the Health Centres that linked idle equipment to a lack of skills, including oxygen equipment users, for example, at Bubago and Sop Sop and staff requiring orientation at Avogera. Handover should include a demonstration, basic maintenance guidance and a named route for technical support. Future supply contracts should make on-site demonstration to the staff who will use the  equipment a condition of  acceptance, and staffing reviews should take account of the equipment each health centre now holds.
0502: p Recommendation. Institution asset managers and LG finance, health and education officers should  apply UgIFT markings where physically appropriate and confirm  asset locations and custodians. Assets carrying another marking only should receive the programme identifier at their next  check, while retaining serials and vehicle registration details. Resolve the continuing allocation of  equipment,  for example at Todora, Paraa and  Masafu, through signed transfer or receipt documentation, bring assets held at district stores to their facilities once rooms are ready, and complete delivery of the items still with  suppliers before recording them as received or in service.
0503: p The Ministry of Works and Transport should retain  its tracing  records and keep the confirmed custodians and locations current.  Suspected losses should be reported and investigated  through  asset-specific  records, and every facility should hold a list of the UgIFT assets it received, showing each item, its  identifier and its custodian. Distinguish delivered assets from planned supplies, missing components and assets temporarily held elsewhere. Maintain site, ownership and handover records for buildings and land.
0504: p Recommendation. The Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development, with the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Education and Sports and the local governments concerned, should approve a corrected programme list after rectifying the inconsistencies. The corrected list should then guide decisions wherever planned locations differed from what teams found.

0525: p Rules for an approved register correction
0526: p Enter a full calendar date only when the evidence shows when the identified asset became available for use. Keep the source’s original wording and level of detail in the supporting record. A month, year, financial year or commissioning status must not be entered as though it were a full date.
0527: p Leave the date field blank in the corrected register where a full date cannot be supported. Record the reason, original value and source reference separately. Keep any work-in-progress or undelivered status visible even when the date is blank.
0528: p Where the source supports an early date, establish whether the asset predates UgIFT or has been assigned to the wrong beneficiary or programme. Do not simply move the year forward or remove the asset from the register. Record the approved decision about its ownership and programme assignment.
0529: p Before approving changes, compare purchase and service dates, check repeated entries and confirm which physical units they describe. A date may be applied to a whole batch only where the documents identify both the batch and the relevant event. Similar item names or dates at another facility are not sufficient evidence.
0530: p For each approved change, record the original and revised values, supporting evidence, decision, responsible officer and review date. Recalculate depreciation, net book value and any recoverable amount based on net book value. Retain independently supported recoverable amounts separately. Check the resulting balances and outstanding exceptions before migration to IFMIS.
